## Start with the risk the source actually measured

Candello analyzed asserted medical professional liability cases from 2014 through 2024 and found that 40% involved a communication factor, up from 30% in its earlier 2009 through 2013 cohort. Communication-related cases also had 39% greater odds of closing with an indemnity payment.

Those figures come from malpractice claims, not from a trial of ClinicalSim or communication training. They establish that communication failures are common in liability data and that the financial consequences reach beyond the training budget.

## Avoid the false precision of a universal ROI

A hospital cannot calculate communication-training ROI from a national claim percentage alone. The local business case depends on who trains, which conversations they practice, how often they practice, and which outcomes the institution can measure without double counting.

The same discipline applies to HCAHPS reimbursement and standardized patient costs. A single national dollar estimate hides differences in hospital payment mix, actor wages, room use, faculty support, and program design. A credible analysis keeps those inputs separate and labels internal estimates as internal.

## Measure what the program can observe

A communication-training program can directly document participation, the cases completed, performance against a named framework, transcript evidence for each score, and change across repeated sessions. Claims, patient experience, and readmissions may matter to the institution, but a training program should not claim it caused those outcomes without an appropriate study design.

ClinicalSim scores each encounter against a published framework or the institution's own standard, quotes the participant's words under every score, and keeps a timestamped record by person, cohort, or unit. That record gives education and quality leaders something concrete to review while longer-term outcome research continues.

## The business case is stronger when it stays honest

Communication risk is large enough without unsupported per-hospital reimbursement estimates or a composite savings headline. Start with the 40% Candello finding, define the local training population, and decide which outcomes can be measured prospectively. That gives a finance or quality team a model it can inspect instead of a number it has to trust on faith.

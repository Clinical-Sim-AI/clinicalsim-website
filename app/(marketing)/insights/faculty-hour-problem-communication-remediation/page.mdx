Remediation takes real faculty time, but the published 29.6-hour mean comes from one clinical reasoning program and should not be presented as a general range for every remediation case.

## What the faculty-hour evidence actually measures

Guerrasio and Aagaard tracked 53 learners with clinical reasoning deficits in a structured remediation program at the University of Colorado. The mean was 29.6 specialist contact hours, including 20.4 hours of face-to-face work and 9.2 hours of email. The study did not measure communication remediation, and it excluded time from program directors, Clinical Competency Committees, coordinators, and legal teams.

That distinction matters. The number is useful evidence that individual remediation can absorb substantial specialist time, but it is not evidence for a 29-to-45-hour or 25-to-75-hour range across competencies and institutions.

## Program directors are asking for a repeatable process

A 2017 CERA survey reached 267 family medicine program directors. Of the respondents, 93% reported at least one resident in remediation during the prior three years, and 50% selected an accessible remediation toolkit as the most important tool for improving the process. Another 22% identified missing documentation as a major challenge (Frazier et al., 2021).

The survey does not isolate communication cases, so it should not be read as a communication-remediation prevalence estimate. It does show that remediation is common in family medicine and that program directors want a more consistent way to identify deficiencies, assign work, and reassess progress.

## Practice and coaching do different jobs

Faculty judgment belongs in a remediation plan. A program director still has to define the concern, review performance, coach the learner, and decide what evidence is sufficient for the CCC. The avoidable burden is using faculty or standardized patient time to facilitate every repetition between those decisions.

ClinicalSim separates those jobs. Learners can complete physician-authored communication scenarios between coaching sessions, and faculty can review the transcript, rubric scores, and longitudinal record before deciding what to address next. The platform extends faculty coaching and standardized patient programs. It does not replace either one.

## Documentation has to show change over time

The CERA survey found that missing documentation often delays or weakens remediation. A useful communication-remediation record should show the assigned skill, the framework used to assess it, what the learner said, and whether the same behavior changed across later attempts.

That is the practical case for structured practice. The goal is not to attach an inflated dollar figure to faculty time. It is to give faculty better evidence before the next coaching session and give the CCC a record it can evaluate.

## What a goals-of-care conversation has to accomplish

A goals-of-care conversation is not a speech about code status. The clinician has to share prognostic information, learn how the patient wants decisions made, understand fears and priorities, discuss tradeoffs, and ask who should be involved. Bernacki and Block identified those tasks in their review of serious illness communication practices.

That is a lot to manage while a patient or family is processing frightening information. A framework can help clinicians remember the work, but remembering the framework is different from carrying out the conversation in real time.

## Training evidence is promising and limited

Chung and colleagues reviewed 20 studies of end-of-life communication training, including six randomized trials. Compared with usual teaching, training was associated with better self-efficacy, knowledge, and communication scores in standardized patient encounters. The authors rated the evidence from very low to low quality and found too little evidence to determine whether training changed patient-level outcomes.

That is the honest evidence position. Programs have support for structured communication training, but they should measure learner behavior directly and avoid claiming that a training tool has improved clinical outcomes without a study designed to test that question.

## Practice should produce evidence a coach can inspect

A useful practice encounter gives the learner a specific case, a named communication framework, and feedback tied to what the learner said. Repetition matters because the learner can work on one behavior at a time, such as stating prognosis plainly, responding to emotion before moving to logistics, or checking whether the plan matches the family's priorities.

ClinicalSim gives learners a private place to rehearse those conversations with AI patients and produces transcript-grounded feedback for review. Faculty and standardized patients still carry the coaching and high-stakes assessment that require human judgment.

## The Scale of the Problem

Communication failures are now a factor in **40% of all malpractice claims**, according to the CRICO/Candello 2025 benchmarking report, up from 30% in their 2015 analysis. These are not rare edge cases. Over a five-year period, communication breakdowns were linked to 1,744 patient deaths and **$1.7 billion in malpractice costs**.

Claims involving communication failures also have **39% greater odds of closing with an indemnity payment** (Candello 2025), and are more than twice as likely to result in payouts exceeding $1 million (Humphrey et al., Journal of Patient Safety 2022).

The leading predictor of whether a physician will be sued is communication, not technical competence. The Hickson/Vanderbilt work (JAMA, 2002) found that malpractice claims cluster among a small minority of physicians, and that the clustering tracks unsolicited patient complaints about communication rather than clinical errors.

## The HCAHPS Financial Risk

Beyond malpractice, communication skills directly affect hospital revenue. The CMS Hospital Value-Based Purchasing Program withholds 2% of all participating hospitals' Medicare payments and redistributes the pool based on performance scores, with patient experience worth 25% of that score. **Five of the eight HCAHPS measures Medicare pays on are communication measures**, about 15.6% of a hospital's total value-based purchasing score (CMS FY2026 IPPS final rule).

About **$1.7 billion** in Medicare payments is redistributed annually through value-based purchasing (CMS FY2026 IPPS final rule). Readmission penalties, which turn on a discharge conversation, run a further $320 million to $563 million a year and reach roughly three quarters of evaluated hospitals (KFF and Definitive Healthcare analyses of CMS data).

## The Training Gap

Despite these stakes, the training infrastructure is remarkably thin. In our national needs assessment of GME program leaders, trainees got only **3 to 4 live practice conversations across years of training**. The ACGME requires residents to demonstrate interpersonal and communication skills as a core competency, and Milestones 2.0 has required every program to grade each resident and fellow on those skills twice a year since 2022. The requirement is universal. The practice volume behind it is not.

Yet most programs still rely on ad-hoc methods: observation, role-modeling, and hoping that learners learn through experience. For low-frequency, high-stakes conversations like end-of-life discussions and error disclosures, a learner can finish training having led only a handful of real ones, and never once with someone scoring how it went.

## The Opportunity

The conversations that carry the highest stakes (telling a parent their child is dying, disclosing a medical error, guiding a family through goals of care) are the ones clinicians are least prepared for. They have rarely had the chance to practice.

Closing this gap requires training methods that are structured, scalable, and available when clinicians need them, beyond the handful of sessions a sim center can schedule each year.
